Fast forward four years later, to three weeks ago. On Monday, April 6, 2026, a device suspected to be a Chinese Autonomous/Unmanned Underwater Vehicle (AUV/UUV) was discovered off the island of Gili Trawangan, Lombok.

With this latest case, six such devices have now been found in Indonesia since 2019 (Yes, turns out I missed some when I wrote my LPDP essay back then). One or two might be a coincidence. But when it happens six times, can we really be sure there's no pattern behind it?

This writing serves as a follow-up and concrete contribution to the issue I first raised back in my 2022 LPDP essay. I will trace the pattern across these six UUV discoveries based on the type and location of each device.

### Notes

Beyond these six incidents, there have been other underwater device discoveries in Indonesia, among them a suspected UUV in Bintan (2017) [^100] and a Streamer Retriever Device (SRD) in Selayar (2022) [^102] [^103]. These other discoveries are not discussed in this article because:

- The type or source of the device could not be reliably confirmed, or
- The device was confirmed not to be a UUV

## Identifying the UUV Types

Among the six discoveries, three types of UUV can be identified: Underwater Glider, Profiling Float, and Sub-buoy. These devices serve two core functions: **collecting subsea data** and **transmitting that collected data** via satellite. The sensors commonly installed on these devices and the data they collect include:

| Sensor | Variables Measured |
|---|---|
| Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) | Device position coordinates, obtained from GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, or BeiDou satellites |
| Acoustic Doppler Current Profiler (ADCP) | Ocean current velocity |
| Conductivity, Temperature, Depth (CTD) | Conductivity (ability to conduct electricity), temperature, and ocean depth |

### Sea Wing (Haiyi) Underwater Glider

{{<img caption="Sea Wing (Haiyi) UUV"
src="/blog/uuv-discoveries/haiyi.jpg" >}}

The first three devices (2019–2020) are strongly suspected to be **Sea Wing (Haiyi)** UUVs, an underwater glider developed by the Shenyang Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences. All three can be identified by three identical features: the sensor configuration at the nose of the device, the wing position on the body, and the long antenna at the tail. The fifth device (2022) closely resembles an underwater glider in form but is not exactly the same as the Sea Wing, its wings are positioned too close to the tail side, unlike the Sea Wing's more centrally positioned wings.

Underwater gliders can travel long distances to collect subsea data, covering tens to hundreds of kilometers. They do not use propellers to move forward. Instead, they move by gliding, controlling their buoyancy.

{{<img caption="Glide motion used to propel an <i>underwater glider</i>"
source="[SeaGlide](http://seaglide.org/about)"
src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/52f69916e4b0393d858e6f9f/1391897542872-AHSFKSCSSGJ9WF7VMAZ4/GliderMecnaicsOverview.png?format=2500w" >}}

Buoyancy is controlled using a buoyancy engine, a pump that transfers fluid from an internal reservoir to an external bladder. When the bladder expands, the vehicle's volume increases and its effective mass becomes lighter than water, causing it to rise to the surface. Conversely, when the bladder deflates, the vehicle sinks. The two wings convert this vertical motion into forward thrust, exactly like an airplane's wings. The full specifications of the Sea Wing UUV is as follows [^107]:

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Dimensions | 22 cm hull diameter, 2 m length, 120 cm wingspan |
| Weight | 65 kg |
| Operating depth | Up to 1,200 m |
| Battery | Primary or secondary lithium |
| Range | > 500 km |
| Communications sensors | RF modem and Iridium satellite |
| Navigation sensors | GPS, altimeter, TCM |
| Scientific sensors | CTD (Conductivity, Temperature, Depth) |

### Profiling Float? (2021)

{{<img caption="Suspected components of the <i>Profiling Float</i> device found on Anambas Island (2021)"
source="[Covert Shores](https://www.hisutton.com/Object-found-on-Indonesian-beach.html)"
src="https://www.hisutton.com/images/China-Sensor-Float-Indonesia-Jan19-2021.jpg" >}}

Several features of the device found in 2021 resemble a profiling float, commonly known as an Argo float [^9]. A profiling float is a device that collects subsea data over an extended period at a specific location. It moves up and down beneath the surface while collecting data at various depth levels. This device **cannot propel itself forward**, it can only drift with ocean currents.

{{<img caption="How a <i>Profiling Float</i> works"
source="[Argo](https://argo.ucsd.edu/how-do-floats-work/)"
src="https://argo.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/361/2020/06/float_cycle_1.png" >}}

However, no specific profiling float model exactly matches the device found on Anambas Island. Furthermore, several features of the discovered device are inconsistent with typical profiling float designs. First, the discovered device used a propeller at its lower end. Profiling floats generally move up and down in the water by controlling buoyancy using a buoyancy engine (the same as an underwater glider), not a propeller. Second, no data-collection sensors such as a CTD were found on the body of the discovered device, unlike the Sea Wing UUV discoveries where the primary sensors were still intact.

Therefore, the theory that the device found in the Anambas Islands (2021) is a profiling float remains questionable. However, the Chinese characters on the device's body strongly suggest that whatever type of device it originally was, it almost certainly originated from China.

### Deep-Sea Real-Time Transmission Mooring System (2026)

{{<img caption="The <i>Deep-Sea Real-Time Transmission Mooring System</i> device from CSIC"
source="[Qingdao Hishun Ocean Equipment Co., Ltd.](https://www.qdhisun.com/pd.jsp?fromColId=106&id=15)"
src="/blog/uuv-discoveries/subbuoy.jpg" >}}

The device found at Gili Trawangan (2026) has been identified as a Sub-buoy or Deep-Sea Real-Time Transmission Mooring System, developed by the 710 / Yichang Research Institute, part of China Shipbuilding Industry Corporation (CSIC). This system has been under testing since 2016 by the Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and at that time successfully transmitted deep-sea data in real time for 190 days from the Western Pacific [^10].

{{<img caption="Diagram of the Deep-Sea Real-Time Transmission Mooring System"
source="Chen, S., Li, X. (2022). Underwater Information Sensing Technology. In: Cui, W., Fu, S., Hu, Z. (eds) Encyclopedia of Ocean Engineering."
src="/blog/uuv-discoveries/subbuoy-diagram.png" >}}

Unlike the profiling float, this device cannot move forward, and unlike the underwater glider, it also cannot move up or down. This device is **completely stationary** and must be installed in a fixed position. It can only be moored to the seabed with an anchor and mooring line, with an endurance of up to 12 months. It can operate at depths of 4,000 meters below the surface, but can be moored at depths of 80–300 m from the surface [^11].

This device carries two ADCPs (300 kHz and 75 kHz), one CTD, and one acoustic sensor. For communication, the device can launch two types of surface buoys: a tethered buoy (retrievable) or an expendable buoy released to the surface to transmit data via satellite [^11].

When the six UUV discovery locations are plotted on a map, no immediately obvious pattern emerges. However, taking into account the Sea Wing underwater glider's range (500 km), a larger pattern becomes visible: the Indonesian Archipelagic Sea Lanes (ALKI). Specifically, the UUVs were found near three ALKI routes: ALKI I, ALKI II, and ALKI IIIA.

> Disclaimer: The device found on Alor Island (2022) is likely not a Sea Wing UUV. However, its range is still assumed to be 500 km for consistency with the other Sea Wing UUVs.

### ALKI

The ALKI regulations are established under Government Regulation No. 37 of 2002, based on Chapter IV of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) 1982. In essence, Indonesia is required to provide designated sea lanes for foreign vessels transiting between international waters.

There are conditions, however. Ships transiting through ALKI must maintain continuous and expeditious transit, may not stop except in emergencies, and must not deviate significantly from the lane's axis (no more than 25 nautical miles or 10% of the distance to the nearest island boundary).

In other words, Chinese ships actually have a legal right to transit through ALKI. This fact is highly relevant to the pattern of UUV discoveries we see.

### Potential Underwater Glider Launch Locations

| Discovery Location | Nearest ALKI Route | Estimated Distance to ALKI |
|---|---|---|
| Poto Island | ALKI I | 179.33 km |
| Masalembu Islands | ALKI II | 290.55 km |
| Selayar Islands | ALKI II | 384.85 km |
| Anambas Islands | ALKI I | 159.87 km |
| Alor Island | ALKI IIIA | 26.88 km |
| Gili Trawangan | ALKI II | 18.20 km |

Notice the dashed orange circles on the map. These circles show the estimated 500 km range radius from each underwater glider discovery point. It is clearly visible that the range areas of these devices overlap with ALKI I, ALKI II, and ALKI IIIA.

Is it possible these devices were launched from a moving ship? **Very likely.** In July 2023, L3Harris successfully demonstrated fully autonomous launch and recovery of a UUV from a moving submarine using a Torpedo Tube Launch and Recovery (TTL&R) system [^2].

Launching a UUV from a surface ship is far simpler than from a submarine. A Launch & Recovery System can be mounted on the stern of a ship for rapid UUV deployment. With such a system, UUVs can be launched while a vessel transits slowly or pauses briefly along an ALKI route.

{{<img caption="The Launch & Recovery System from HII used to deploy the REMUS AUV/UUV."
source="[Naval News](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2026/01/hii-successfully-demonstrates-sea-launcher-ship-based-automated-launch-and-recovery-of-remus-auv/)"
src="https://www.navalnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/hii-remus-scaled.png.webp" >}}

The plausible scenario is therefore: a vessel legally transits ALKI, and en route, deploys a Sea Wing UUV. The UUV then carries out its mission autonomously for days or weeks. Upon completion, it surfaces to transmit data via satellite. At the surface, the UUV can either be recovered by a ship or left to drift with ocean currents.

The 500 km range from each discovery point also covers areas beyond Indonesia's territorial waters but still within Indonesia's Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ). The EEZ is the area extending 200 nautical miles from Indonesia's territorial sea. Foreign vessels are permitted to operate in the EEZ, but deploying military intelligence equipment in another nation's EEZ, including UUVs, remains a grey area in international law.

### Long-Term Monitoring Devices

If underwater gliders like the Sea Wing can still be launched from a distance, the same cannot be said for the sub-buoy and profiling float. Because these two devices cannot move on their own, they must be **installed directly at their operating location** by a ship. This means the vessel carrying them must have been physically present near the discovery coordinates, not simply transiting legally along an ALKI route.

The Gili Trawangan discovery site is only about **18.2 km from ALKI II**, the closest any discovery has been to an ALKI route. Meanwhile, the Anambas device, though approximately 160 km from ALKI I, was only about **61 km from the boundary of Indonesia's territorial sea**. That 61 km is still within Indonesia's EEZ, but as I explained above, foreign vessels are still permitted to transit EEZ waters.

Both profiling floats and sub-buoys are designed for **continuous, long-duration data collection** at a fixed point. The discovery of these two stationary devices implies that China is not only conducting **initial surveys** with underwater gliders, but also **long-term monitoring**.

### Why Near ALKI?

ALKI I differs dramatically from ALKI II and IIIA in underwater characteristics. The average depth along ALKI I is only 40–100 meters. By contrast, ALKI II and ALKI IIIA reach depths of 2,000 meters.

In addition, these deep-water lanes are the primary corridors of the **Indonesian Throughflow (ITF)**, the current system that transfers water from the Pacific Ocean to the Indian Ocean. The meeting and movement of water masses at different temperatures creates thick thermocline layers (extreme temperature gradients) and halocline layers (extreme salinity gradients) along the ITF corridor [^12].

When acoustic waves from a surface ship's sonar strike these thermocline layers, the sound refracts and bends downward. This extreme refraction creates a phenomenon called the ***shadow zone***, a "blind spot" in the water column where surface sonar simply cannot reach [^14]. This is the safest place for a submarine to maneuver without being detected by sonar.

{{<img caption="Visualization of sonar wave refraction in the underwater shadow zone."
source="[Naval Gazing](https://www.navalgazing.net/Sound-in-the-Ocean)"
src="https://www.navalgazing.net/attach/SurfaceDuct.png?v=1653056267.png" >}}

Basically, the perfect place for a submarine to move undetected (not like they're easily detectable to begin with).

So what valuable data can be obtained from devices placed in these strategic waters? Ocean current velocity and direction profiles, thermocline depth maps, and internal wave characteristics at various deep-sea chokepoints. All of this data can be collected using the CTD and ADCP sensors found on the discovered UUVs. Accurate thermocline mapping is the "master key" for submarines to determine their optimal route and operating depth to always safely maneuver within the shadow zone.

## Conclusion

Over the past seven years, at least six suspected Chinese UUVs have been found within 18–385 km of ALKI routes I, II, and IIIA. All UUVs were discovered by fishermen near island coastlines and/or near the sea surface.

Analysis of the UUV types reveals that:

- UUVs of the underwater glider type were found within a feasible range (<500 km) to have been launched from vessels transiting legally through ALKI and Indonesia's EEZ. This type of UUV is designed to move by gliding over long distances to collect data.

- UUVs of the profiling float and sub-buoy types needed to be deployed directly near their discovery locations by a ship. Both were found at locations close to ALKI routes and Indonesia's EEZ. This type of UUV is designed for long-term data collection at a fixed point.

Based on the CTD and ADCP sensors found on the discovered UUVs, the data being collected includes ocean temperature and current velocity. This data can be used to map shadow zone routes along ALKI,particularly ALKI II and IIIA, enabling submarine movements that are difficult to detect.

## Reflections

It is possible these devices were used not for military intelligence operations, but for civilian oceanographic research. Perhaps all the UUV discoveries at strategic locations around ALKI are just a coincidence.

Maybe, just maybe, this is not the tip of the iceberg. Surely there couldn't be many more UUVs out there that have not yet been accidentally found by our fishermen, right?

Geopolitical implications aside, as an engineer, I can't help but be impressed.

China has developed its underwater robotics and submarine capabilities remarkably well. Its underwater robotics capacity is no longer limited to securing its own waters, it is now proactively mapping strategic sea areas beyond its own borders.

On the other hand, Indonesia, blessed with 5.8 million km² of waters and ALKI routes that serve as strategic global shipping lanes; has very, very limited underwater robotics capabilities.

It has been six years since I started pursuing this field seriously. There is still so much I have not yet mastered. From the very beginning, I have believed that underwater robotics is not just a cool research topic, it's a matter of national security.

Can we truly be responsible for our oceans, and everything within, that is bestowed upon our nation, when another country is more serious and capable of "researching" them?
